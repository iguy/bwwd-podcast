What does Mormon history look like when we include the relationships and experiences so often left out? In this episode of *Better World With Design*, I review D. Michael Quinn’s 1996 book *Same-Sex Dynamics among Nineteenth-Century Americans: A Mormon Example*. I walk through Quinn’s case for widening what historians count as a relationship — friendship, shared households, romantic and erotic bonds — and look closely at Louie B. Felt and May Anderson, Evan Stephens, Ada Dwyer Russell, and Mildred Berryman’s study of a Salt Lake community. I weigh what the evidence can and can’t prove, who is missing from the archive, and how a book from 1996 reads today.
